Context

Why the nanoscale matters

Biology already works at 1–100 nm, so nanomedicine speaks the body's own language.

  • DNA helix: about 2 nm wide
  • Proteins: 2–10 nm
  • Viruses: 20–200 nm
  • Nanoparticles can cross barriers larger objects cannot
  • High surface-to-volume ratio: more drug, more binding sites
Tools

The nanocarrier toolbox

  • Liposomes: fat bubbles holding water- or fat-soluble drugs
  • Lipid nanoparticles: protect fragile RNA
  • Polymeric nanoparticles: biodegradable, slow release
  • Gold & iron-oxide particles: imaging and heating
  • Dendrimers & quantum dots: branched carriers, fluorescent labels
Treatment

Targeted drug delivery

The goal: put the drug exactly where it is needed.

  • Passive targeting: leaky tumor vessels let nanoparticles accumulate (EPR effect)
  • Active targeting: antibodies on the surface bind receptors of diseased cells
  • Triggered release: pH, heat or light opens the carrier
  • Example: Doxil, liposomal doxorubicin, lowers heart toxicity
Treatment

mRNA vaccines

Lipid nanoparticles made rapid COVID-19 vaccines possible.

  • mRNA alone is destroyed quickly in the body
  • A lipid shell of about 80–100 nm protects it
  • Cells take it up and read the instructions
  • They make a harmless protein; the immune system learns it
  • Same platform studied for flu, cancer and gene editing
Treatment

Fighting cancer

  • Photothermal therapy: gold nanoshells absorb near-infrared light and heat tumors
  • Magnetic hyperthermia: iron-oxide particles heated by a magnetic field
  • Albumin-bound paclitaxel (Abraxane) approved for breast and pancreatic cancer
  • Radio-enhancers: hafnium oxide particles boost radiotherapy
  • Healthy tissue is spared more than with classic chemotherapy
Diagnosis

Nano-diagnostics & imaging

  • MRI contrast: iron-oxide nanoparticles reveal liver and lymph node lesions
  • Quantum dots: bright, stable fluorescent tags for cells
  • Gold nanoparticle tests: the color change in lateral-flow tests, such as pregnancy tests
  • Biosensors: detect a few molecules of a cancer marker in a drop of blood
  • Earlier detection means better survival
Repair

Regenerative medicine

  • Nano-scaffolds mimic the extracellular matrix to guide cell growth
  • Nano-hydroxyapatite in bone grafts and dental implants
  • Nerve repair: aligned nanofibers guide regrowing axons
  • Wound care: silver nanoparticle dressings fight infection
  • Growth factors released slowly from nanoparticles

✔ Advantages

  • Precise delivery, fewer side effects
  • Lower drug doses needed
  • Earlier and more sensitive diagnosis
  • Protects fragile drugs such as RNA
  • Combines imaging and therapy
  • Enables tissue repair

✘ Disadvantages

  • Long-term toxicity still unclear
  • Particles may accumulate in liver or spleen
  • Hard to manufacture consistently
  • High R&D and treatment costs
  • Complex regulation and approval
  • Ethical and privacy concerns

Conclusion

Nanomedicine: smaller tools,
bigger hope for patients.
